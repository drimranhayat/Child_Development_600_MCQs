# 1
Which set matches the lecture's three components of emotion?~Subjective feeling, physiological change and overt behavior~Height, weight and head circumference~Vocabulary, spelling and handwriting~Grasp, release and wrist rotation~Emotion includes felt experience, bodily changes and observable behavior. These are related aspects, not three separate diagnoses.
A child feels frightened, has a racing heart and clings to an adult. Which component is clinging?~Overt behavior~A body-size measure~A linguistic unit~The subjective feeling itself~Clinging is an observable action. Fear is the subjective experience, while changes such as a racing heart concern physiology.
Which ability most directly reflects emotional understanding?~Recognizing why a friend might feel disappointed~Repeating a word without meaning~Measuring a friend's height~Copying a square accurately~Emotional understanding links feelings with situations and perspectives. It involves more than simply recognizing a facial shape or repeating an emotion word.
What is empathy concerned with?~Understanding and responding to another person's feelings~Experiencing no personal emotion~Winning every disagreement~Copying every action automatically~Empathy involves awareness of another person's experience and an appropriate response. Comforting an upset peer can be one expression of it.
Why should emotion and behavior be distinguished?~A feeling can be expressed through different actions~One feeling always produces one exact action~Behavior reveals every private thought~Feelings have no influence on action~A disappointed child might cry, ask for help or withdraw. Supporting a better response does not require denying the feeling.
What additional capacity is especially relevant to self-conscious emotions?~Awareness of oneself and social evaluation~A newborn grasp reflex~A specific tooth count~Independent stair climbing alone~Pride, shame and guilt involve how the self or one's actions relate to standards and others' responses. They require more than basic bodily arousal.
A child is angry after losing a game. Which response separates feeling from action?~Acknowledge anger while preventing hitting~Punish the existence of anger itself~Allow any action because anger is real~Insist that the child must feel happy~Adults can validate a feeling and maintain a behavioral boundary together. The child then has a chance to learn a safer way to express distress.
# 2
How does a reflexive smile differ from a social smile?~It need not be a response to a social partner~It always conveys a deliberate joke~It requires spoken conversation~It proves a fully developed attachment pattern~A spontaneous early smile need not follow a face or voice. A socially responsive smile occurs within interaction.
Why can a social smile strengthen caregiver engagement?~It creates a rewarding reciprocal exchange~It completes every aspect of emotional regulation~It eliminates all later crying~It proves that strangers are always preferred~A caregiver may feel responded to and continue the exchange. Such repeated mutual participation contributes to the developing relationship.
What does increasing contextual sensitivity of emotion mean?~Responses become more related to the meaning of a situation~Every situation produces an identical expression~Feelings become unrelated to experience~Only physical size affects emotion~As cognition and experience develop, the significance of an event matters increasingly. An unfamiliar situation may produce a different response from a familiar one.
A baby distinguishes two facial displays in a study. What remains uncertain without further evidence?~Whether the baby understands their emotional significance~Whether the displays look different~Whether visual attention occurred~Whether a stimulus was presented~Discriminating appearances and understanding emotions are different claims. A looking response alone should not be stretched into adult-like emotional knowledge.
Why should exact crying-duration figures not be used alone to judge health?~Crying varies and must be interpreted with other signs~Crying has no relation to needs~Every baby has the same daily pattern~Any crying proves an attachment disorder~Duration can be one observation, but feeding, behavior, age and clinical circumstances also matter. A slide average cannot replace assessment of a concern.
Which statement about the onset of fear is most cautious?~Fear-related responses develop with variation and context~No infant can show fear before one fixed month~Every child develops identical fears simultaneously~Fear appears only after fluent language~The slide's exact-month claims are too rigid. Responses to threat, novelty and unfamiliar people should be interpreted in developmental context.
What is the main problem with inferring 'anger at a particular person' from newborn crying?~The inference exceeds what crying alone establishes~Newborns never experience distress~Crying is always deliberate punishment~A facial display supplies a complete explanation~Newborn distress can be observed without establishing a sophisticated attribution of blame. Behavior should not be assigned adult-like reasoning without evidence.
# 3
Which sequence matches Bowlby's four-phase teaching model?~Pre-attachment, attachment-in-the-making, clear-cut attachment, reciprocal relationship~Clear-cut attachment, pre-attachment, reciprocal relationship, attachment-in-the-making~Reciprocal relationship, clear-cut attachment, pre-attachment, attachment-in-the-making~Attachment-in-the-making, reciprocal relationship, clear-cut attachment, pre-attachment~The model moves from early signaling toward selective relationships and later partnership. Phases are approximate descriptions rather than compulsory deadlines.
What is characteristic of the pre-attachment phase in the model?~Signals that bring caregivers into contact~Adult-like negotiation about a separation~An abstract account of relationship rights~A requirement to identify only one possible caregiver~Early signals such as crying and clinging attract care. The model does not require an already mature selective relationship at that point.
What change is central to attachment-in-the-making?~Increasing preference for familiar people~Loss of all response to caregivers~Completion of formal reasoning~Exclusive interest in objects instead of people~Familiarity and repeated care become increasingly important. The infant begins to form expectations within these interactions.
What is meant by a caregiver acting as a secure base?~A trusted point from which the child can explore~A guarantee that the child never leaves the caregiver~A reason to prevent all exploration~A score based only on feeding frequency~Security supports exploration as well as comfort seeking. A toddler may play independently while checking back with a familiar adult.
Which ability helps the later reciprocal phase?~Growing language and understanding of others' goals~The return of newborn head lag~The disappearance of every emotion~A fixed adult height~Better communication and perspective-taking can support simple negotiation. For example, a toddler can understand a brief explanation about a caregiver's return.
A child asks when a parent will return and agrees on a goodbye routine. Which phase does this most resemble?~A more reciprocal relationship~Pre-attachment signaling alone~Absence of all attachment~A purely reflexive feeding pattern~The child is participating more actively in managing separation. This fits the model's emphasis on growing partnership rather than only immediate contact seeking.
Why should the phases not be used as exact diagnostic age cutoffs?~They are broad descriptions within a theoretical model~All phases are directly measured by height~Every child follows an identical day-by-day schedule~Later relationships are unaffected by experience~Development and circumstances vary. The framework organizes patterns but cannot establish a diagnosis merely from one age label.
# 4
Which pattern best supports trust in a caregiving relationship?~Repeated sensitive responses to needs~One gift followed by persistent unresponsiveness~Unpredictable punishment for every signal~Perfect performance on a vocabulary test~Relationships build through repeated experience. Predictable, responsive care helps a child learn what to expect from a caregiver.
Why should early-deprivation studies not be interpreted as proving later support is useless?~Evidence of vulnerability does not establish impossibility of later change~Early experience has no importance~Animal findings automatically predict every human outcome~Support always guarantees complete recovery~Early conditions can have lasting influence, but later experiences and support can still matter. Avoid turning vulnerability into an absolute claim about every child.
A child is friendly with many adults. What would better inform attachment assessment?~Patterns of comfort seeking and relationship use~The number of people greeted once~Only the child's height percentile~A preference for colourful toys~General friendliness is not the whole attachment relationship. How the child uses familiar people during stress and exploration provides different information.
Can a child have meaningful attachment relationships with more than one caregiver?~Yes, repeated care can support multiple relationships~No, every additional caregiver prevents attachment~Only a biological parent can matter~Only the first person after delivery can matter~Person-specific bonds can develop with more than one trusted caregiver. Their quality depends on repeated interactions and the child's circumstances.
What should be inferred from one difficult separation?~More context is needed to understand the relationship~The attachment must be insecure~The child cannot tolerate any later change~The caregiver has caused a permanent disorder~Fatigue, novelty and the situation can affect separation behavior. One episode does not establish a full relationship classification.
Why is caregiver wellbeing relevant to responsive care?~Stress and health can affect available attention and support~Only the infant's characteristics influence interaction~Wellbeing guarantees every response is ideal~Caregiver distress has no need for care itself~Caregivers also need support. Addressing their needs can improve the practical conditions for repeated sensitive interaction.
Which approach is best when a child enters a new caregiving arrangement?~Build predictable routines and responsive relationships~Assume trust must be instant~Treat all distress as defiance~Avoid comfort so independence develops faster~Trust can grow through consistent experience. Gradual, responsive support respects both the child's history and capacity for later relationship development.

What is goodness of fit in temperament?~The match between a child's characteristics and environmental demands~A ranking of children by obedience~The absence of individual differences~A body-mass calculation~A good fit adapts expectations and support to the child. It does not require every child to have the same temperament.
A highly active toddler struggles during a long quiet wait. Which adjustment improves fit?~Provide safe movement breaks and realistic expectations~Interpret activity as a moral defect~Expect unlimited stillness immediately~Remove all opportunities for movement~The child's characteristics interact with the demand. Adjusting the environment can support participation while appropriate limits remain.
A child is slow to approach unfamiliar adults. What is a supportive interpretation?~A cautious style may require time to warm up~The child is necessarily hostile~The child has no capacity for friendship~A single encounter proves an attachment diagnosis~Cautious approach is an individual response pattern. Familiarity and patient introduction may help without assigning a fixed negative label.
What does reactivity refer to broadly?~The intensity or pattern of response to stimulation~A measure of adult height~The number of primary teeth~The length of a written sentence~Reactivity concerns how a person responds to events or stimulation. It can vary alongside developing regulation and experience.
Why is 'difficult child' a limited temperament description?~It can confuse a poor fit with a defect in the child~It is a complete standardized diagnosis~It explains every possible behavior~It replaces observation of context~The same characteristic may work differently in different settings. Describe specific responses and demands rather than treating the child as the entire problem.
Which observation helps distinguish a characteristic response from a single mood?~A recurring pattern across relevant situations~One brief reaction when ill~A single school photograph~One adult's first impression~Temperament refers to relatively characteristic patterns. Repeated observation provides stronger evidence than an isolated reaction influenced by temporary circumstances.
Why should biologically influenced temperament not be called completely unchangeable behavior?~Expression is shaped by maturation, experience and fit~Biological influences are imaginary~Every child eventually becomes identical~Context cannot alter participation~Underlying differences can be meaningful without fixing every future response. Support and changing demands influence how characteristics are expressed.
# 6
How does separation distress differ from stranger wariness?~One concerns caregiver departure; the other concerns unfamiliar people~Both always mean exactly the same event~Separation distress is a dental process~Stranger wariness requires written language~The situations differ even when both involve uncertainty. Clear descriptions avoid merging all fearful behavior into one category.
Why might growing mobility increase the relevance of caregiver checking?~The child can explore farther from a familiar base~Movement removes the need for relationships~Crawling proves adult-like risk judgment~Attachment exists only before any movement~Mobility creates new opportunities for distance and novelty. Checking back can help the child manage exploration and uncertainty.
A child hesitates at a noisy toy and watches a parent's reaction. Which information is being sought?~An emotional cue about the situation~An exact calculation of bone age~A measure of the toy's weight~A written command to repeat~Social referencing uses another person's reaction as information. It is particularly relevant when the meaning or safety of an event is uncertain.
Why is social referencing not equivalent to a full theory of mind?~Using an emotional cue is narrower than reasoning about mental states~Both concepts refer only to facial muscles~Social referencing requires no other person~Theory of mind means identifying body size~The abilities are related but not identical. Looking to a caregiver for guidance does not establish every aspect of understanding beliefs or perspectives.
Which goodbye routine may support a child facing separation?~A predictable, reassuring departure suited to the situation~An unexplained disappearance every time~Promises that are repeatedly not kept~Punishment for seeking comfort~Predictability can make the event easier to understand. Support should reflect the child's age and circumstances rather than requiring immediate absence of distress.
What should be considered when stranger wariness appears stronger in one setting?~Familiarity, fatigue and interaction context~Only the child's birth weight~A certain diagnosis from one encounter~The assumption that every setting is equivalent~Behavior can vary with the demands and state of the child. A crowded unfamiliar room differs from gradual contact in a familiar place.
Which conclusion about separation distress is most responsible?~It can occur within otherwise supportive relationships~It always proves poor caregiving~It means object recognition is absent~It prevents every form of exploration~Distress at separation is not automatically pathology. Consider intensity, persistence, context and everyday functioning when a concern is raised.

What does delaying gratification require?~Waiting for a desired outcome instead of acting immediately~Recognizing only a colour difference~Repeating a vowel with no delay~Growing a first permanent tooth~Waiting draws on regulation and understanding. Why is complex reasoning often ineffective during peak distress?~The child may be less able to attend and process it~The child can never learn any rule~All language skills disappear permanently~Only punishment can change behavior~High arousal can limit immediate processing. What is an example of external behavioral support?~An adult calmly guiding the next step~A child independently planning an entire routine~An adolescent evaluating an abstract principle~A child silently checking a self-made plan~External regulation comes from another person or structure. Why should tantrum frequency or duration not be a standalone diagnosis?~Patterns must be considered with context, harm and functioning~Duration can never be observed~Every tantrum is a disorder~Only the child's shoe size matters~Repeated severe difficulties may deserve assessment, but a numerical rule alone is insufficient. Which response best supports learning after a child has calmed?~Practise a simple alternative way to request help~Repeat blame without teaching an alternative~Assume no later discussion can help~Promise that frustration will never recur~A calm period allows modelling and practice.
