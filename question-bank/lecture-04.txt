# 1
Why is a newborn's head relatively prominent compared with later childhood?~Body proportions change with development~The trunk never grows after birth~The face is always larger than the skull~Limb length reaches its adult value at birth~Infants and older children have different body proportions. Which set best describes measurements used to follow infant growth?~Weight, recumbent length and head circumference~Vocabulary, gestures and sentence length~Puberty stage, exam score and grip style~Peer ratings, copying skill and sleep state~These measures follow different physical dimensions. What does subcutaneous fat refer to?~Fat beneath the skin~The mineralized outer layer of a tooth~The central cavity of a bone~The coating around a neural axon~Subcutaneous means under the skin. It is part of body composition and should not be confused with dental enamel or neural myelin.
Why can a smaller infant's single weight not establish poor development?~Gestational age, growth pattern and function also matter~Weight is never useful~Small infants cannot learn new skills~All infants should have identical weight~One measurement lacks the full context. Interpretation includes age, previous growth, health, feeding and developmental observations.
A student's notes say 'birth weight triples by one year'. Why is regaining weight after early newborn loss assessed over time?~The direction and context of change are important~One day always determines the entire trajectory~Every loss has the same cause~Later measurements add no information~Monitoring shows whether weight is recovering and how feeding is going. What does deceleration of growth rate mean?~Growth continues but the increase per unit time becomes smaller~The child necessarily loses height~All physical development has stopped~Body measurements become impossible~A slower rate does not mean no growth. Which interpretation of head growth is most appropriate?~Its trajectory adds information distinct from weight~It is identical to vocabulary growth~One large reading proves hydrocephalus~It replaces assessment of all other body dimensions~Head circumference follows a different dimension from weight or length. What does genu varum describe?~Bow-legged alignment~Knock-kneed alignment~Forward curvature of the neck alone~Delayed eruption of incisors~Varum and valgum describe different alignment patterns. Age-related variation should be distinguished from persistent or symptomatic concerns.
Which finding makes a simple 'normal age variation' explanation less sufficient?~Persistent marked asymmetry or pain~A broad range of comfortable activities~A changing appetite with steady growth~A proportion change without other concern~Symptoms or marked asymmetry warrant closer assessment. What typically becomes more developed during middle-childhood motor activity?~Strength, stamina and trained coordination~Only primitive feeding reflexes~Only the capacity for passive movement~A universal adult level of endurance~School-age children can practise increasingly complex activities. A child grows steadily but is shorter than one classmate. What contributes to voice changes during puberty in the lecture?~Growth of the larynx and related structures~Eruption of the first primary incisor~Loss of the palmar grasp response~A decrease in all respiratory structures~Pubertal changes in the larynx and vocal apparatus affect the voice. # 4
How many teeth are in the complete primary dentition?~20~16~28~32~Primary dentition has 20 teeth. The complete permanent set may include 32 when third molars are present, so the two dentitions should be distinguished.
What does tooth eruption mean?~A tooth emerging into the mouth through the gum~The formation of a neural connection~Loss of a primary tooth only~Measurement of skeletal age~Eruption concerns a tooth becoming visible in the mouth. It is distinct from mineralization and from shedding.
What does exfoliation mean in dental development?~Shedding of primary teeth~Initial mineralization of enamel~Emergence of the first fetal limb bud~Formation of the jaw before birth~Exfoliation is the loss of primary teeth as dentition changes. It should not be confused with eruption of a new tooth.
Which teeth often begin primary eruption?~Lower central incisors~Third permanent molars~Second permanent premolars~Upper wisdom teeth~Lower central incisors commonly appear early in primary eruption. Individual timing varies, and a pattern should not be reduced to one exact month.
Why should a first permanent molar not be mistaken for a replacement primary molar?~It erupts behind the primary molars~It is always a temporary tooth~It replaces a primary incisor~It appears only after all wisdom teeth~The first permanent molar is an addition behind the primary teeth. Recognizing this helps distinguish permanent teeth from teeth that are expected to shed.
What is tooth mineralization?~Deposition of mineral in developing dental tissues~The shedding of every primary tooth~The production of a spoken consonant~The measurement of limb length~Mineralization is part of tooth formation. A tooth may be developing before it erupts visibly into the mouth.
Why can enamel changes require a broader history?~Illness, exposures and nutritional factors can contribute~Every discoloured tooth proves one endocrine disorder~Enamel colour directly measures intelligence~Only chronological age can affect enamel~Several influences may affect developing dental tissues. Appearance alone does not establish one specific cause.
Why is tooth eruption a limited proxy for overall development?~Its timing does not track every domain closely~It directly measures language and cognition~It determines attachment security~It replaces height and weight monitoring~Dental development is one physical process. A child's tooth pattern cannot establish communication, learning or social functioning.

What is the difference between chronological age and skeletal age?~One records elapsed time; the other estimates bone maturity~Both always refer to the same number~Skeletal age measures emotional regulation~Chronological age is determined by a hand radiograph~Chronological age is time since birth. What are epiphyses in the context of skeletal maturation?~Developing end regions of long bones~The outer crowns of teeth~Communication junctions between neurons~The infant's sleep states~Epiphyseal development contributes to skeletal maturation. What does an ossification centre indicate?~An area where bone tissue is forming~A place where language is stored exclusively~The loss of a primary tooth~A new stage of attachment~Ossification centres are part of bone development. Their appearance and maturation contribute to reference-based estimates of skeletal age.
Why does bone-age estimation require reference standards?~Observed maturation needs a comparison framework~Every hand image gives a self-evident exact age~Reference populations never matter~Bone maturity is identical to height~A reference provides a structured comparison. Interpretation must consider the method, population and clinical question rather than treating an image as an infallible clock.
A child's skeletal age differs from chronological age. What is the best next interpretation?~Consider the difference alongside growth and clinical history~Assume an exact diagnosis from the difference~Disregard all other evidence~Use it as an intelligence score~The difference can be informative but is not a diagnosis by itself. Growth pattern, puberty and health history help explain its significance.
Why can skeletal maturity help interpret pubertal growth?~It provides information about progress toward physical maturity~It reveals the exact date of the first word~It measures the number of friendships~It replaces all pubertal examination~Skeletal age and puberty concern related aspects of maturation. What is a limitation of adult-height prediction from bone maturity?~It is an estimate rather than a guarantee~It always gives the exact final centimetre~It does not use any reference information~It proves the cause of every growth difference~Predictions depend on assumptions and measurement methods. Which use of skeletal assessment is appropriate?~A clinically justified question interpreted by trained professionals~Routine home imaging to compare siblings~A substitute for all functional assessment~A method of ranking children's character~Imaging and interpretation belong to clinical decision-making.
