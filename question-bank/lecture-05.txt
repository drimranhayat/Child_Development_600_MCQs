# 1
Which example is nonlinguistic communication?~Leaning away and frowning to signal discomfort~Writing a grammatical sentence~Signing a sentence in a signed language~Reading a written instruction~Posture and facial expression can communicate without a linguistic structure. Signed languages, unlike simple body signals, are full language systems.
Which example is paralinguistic information?~A change in vocal tone conveying irritation~The dictionary meaning of a noun~The spelling of a written word~The number of letters in a name~Tone can modify how a message is interpreted. The same words may sound reassuring or impatient depending on their delivery.
Why is a signed language not simply a collection of nonlinguistic gestures?~It has organized linguistic structures and meanings~It contains no grammar~It cannot express abstract ideas~It is only an emotional facial display~Signed languages are linguistic systems. The visual modality does not make their structured messages less linguistic than speech.
What is a communicative modality?~A channel or form used to convey a message~A guaranteed level of intelligence~The age at the first permanent tooth~The number of words in one textbook~Messages can be communicated through speech, signs, writing and other channels. Modality concerns the form used, not the total developmental ability.
A child says 'fine' while sounding upset. What explains the listener's interpretation?~Words and paralinguistic cues are considered together~The word's meaning alone proves contentment~Tone cannot contribute to meaning~All messages require writing~Listeners use tone and context alongside words. A mismatch between verbal content and emotional delivery may prompt clarification.
Which description best captures communication as an exchange?~A message is produced and interpreted within interaction~Only sound production matters~Only one person's vocabulary size matters~A message must always be a complete sentence~Communication includes expressing and understanding information. A meaningful request can be conveyed by a word, gesture or other accessible signal.
Which situation demonstrates a communicative function beyond requesting?~Pointing to share interest in a passing train~Reaching only to obtain a drink~Pulling an adult's hand only to open a box~Pushing a cup forward only for a refill~Communication can share attention and experience, not just obtain things. Pointing at a train can invite another person to notice it together.
Why is context important when interpreting a gesture?~The same movement can serve different purposes~Every gesture has one universal meaning~Context changes body size immediately~Gesture meaning depends only on hand strength~A raised hand could request a turn, greet someone or signal stopping. The interaction helps determine the intended message.
# 2
Which component is primarily concerned with producing speech sounds?~Articulation~Reading comprehension~Lexical meaning~Narrative organization~Articulation involves movements used to form speech sounds. Clear articulation and understanding language are related but distinct areas.
What does phonation involve?~Generating voice through vocal-fold activity~Interpreting a written paragraph~Organizing a story plot~Choosing a conversational topic~Phonation concerns voice production. Speech also requires coordinated articulation and other processes beyond generating voice.
A child follows 'bring your shoes' but does not yet say those words. What does this show?~Comprehension can exceed spoken expression~Understanding requires identical spoken output~The child has no language ability~Only articulation has been observed~Receptive and expressive skills can differ. Understanding an instruction may appear before the child produces an equivalent phrase.
Which pair correctly matches a written-language process?~Reading with comprehension; writing with expression~Reading with articulation; writing with hearing detection~Reading with phonation; writing with rooting~Reading with motor balance; writing with sound localization~Reading involves interpreting written language, while writing produces it. The slide's repeated phrase about understanding should not define written production.
A child knows what to say but a speech movement is unclear. Which distinction is relevant?~Message formulation versus speech production~Weight versus height~Moral reasoning versus attachment~Tooth eruption versus bone age~Language formulation and spoken execution are distinguishable. Difficulty with speech clarity does not by itself show absent understanding of the message.
What does vocabulary refer to in language assessment?~Words or lexical items understood or used~The loudness of every utterance~The speed of walking~The exact length of a vocal fold~Vocabulary may be considered receptively or expressively. Knowing a word's meaning and using it appropriately provide different evidence.
Which observation mainly concerns grammar?~Combining words with tense and agreement patterns~Turning toward a bell~Detecting a faint tone~Maintaining sitting balance~Grammar concerns how language is organized. Tense, pronouns and combinations can show developing structures beyond a list of isolated words.
Why should language production include more than speech sounds?~It also involves expressing organized meanings~Speech sounds alone establish comprehension~Only pronunciation carries a message~Expression excludes signs and writing~Producing language requires meaningful organization, not merely making sounds. A signed or written message can also demonstrate expression.
# 3
How does auditory detection differ from sound localization?~Detection notices sound; localization identifies where it comes from~Detection interprets stories; localization measures vocabulary~Both mean producing a speech sound~Localization is possible only through reading~The abilities answer different questions: whether a sound occurred and where it originated. A child may detect a sound without locating it accurately.
What does auditory discrimination concern?~Distinguishing differences between sounds~Producing every consonant perfectly~Reading all printed words~Maintaining visual balance~Discrimination involves noticing differences, such as two speech sounds. Detection alone does not establish that the sounds can be distinguished.
Why is listening more than hearing a sound?~It draws on attention, memory and interpretation~It excludes cognition~It is identical to ear size~It always requires spoken repetition~Listening involves selecting and interpreting acoustic information. A child may hear words but struggle to retain a long instruction.
A child responds to a bell but cannot find its source. Which ability is demonstrated most clearly?~Sound detection~Accurate localization~Sentence comprehension~Narrative recall~The response shows that the sound was noticed. Locating the source is a separate demand and has not yet been demonstrated.
What is an auditory feedback loop in early sound play?~Hearing one's own vocal output and adjusting it~Seeing another child's written work~Learning a hand movement without sensory input~A measurement of head circumference~Infants can hear their own sounds while experimenting. This feedback is one contributor to developing vocal control and learning.
Why can background noise make a spoken classroom task harder?~It increases demands on selecting the relevant signal~It changes the child's chronological age~It guarantees a permanent hearing disorder~It makes vocabulary knowledge irrelevant~Listening in noise can demand more attention and processing. Improving the environment may support access without establishing any particular diagnosis.
Which statement about infant auditory ability is most appropriate?~Early responsiveness coexists with continuing maturation~Newborn hearing is identical to adult processing in every respect~No sound is processed before school entry~Auditory experience has no later relevance~Responding to sound early does not equal fully mature processing. The lecture describes continuing development in perception and higher pathways.
Why can listener expectations influence auditory interpretation?~Prior knowledge helps make sense of incoming sound~Expectations replace all acoustic evidence~A listener never uses context~Only sound intensity contributes to comprehension~Listening integrates signal and knowledge. In a familiar routine, context can help interpret a partly unclear spoken message.
# 4
What is cooing most closely associated with?~Early vowel-like vocal play~Fully organized written sentences~A mature explanation of a story~A stable adult consonant inventory~Cooing is early vocal activity often described as vowel-like. It is an important developmental behavior without being equivalent to meaningful words.
Which sequence is an example of repeated consonant–vowel babbling?~Ba-ba-ba~A written paragraph~A silent point~A single breath without voicing~Repeated syllable patterns combine consonant-like and vowel-like elements. Babbling is distinguished from the consistent use of a word with meaning.
A baby produces 'mama' during sound play without directing it to anyone. What should be established before calling it a word?~Consistent communicative meaning in context~Perfect adult pronunciation~The ability to spell the sequence~The presence of every adult consonant~A recognizable sound sequence is not automatically a meaningful word. How it is used matters more than similarity to an adult form alone.
What are suprasegmental aspects of early vocalization?~Patterns of pitch, rhythm and stress~Only the number of teeth~Only the child's finger movements~The size of printed letters~Suprasegmentals extend across individual sounds. Early vocal play may vary in pitch and intonation before clear words emerge.
What is a protoconversational exchange?~An early back-and-forth using gaze, sound or gesture~A fully grammatical adult debate~A silent standardized hearing score~A test of permanent tooth growth~Early exchanges resemble conversational turn-taking without requiring words. A caregiver can answer a coo and pause for another response.
A child waves when a familiar person says goodbye. What is demonstrated?~A learned social gesture linked with a routine~Proof of fluent sentence production~A measure of vocal-fold vibration~A complete reading system~Gestural participation can show understanding of a familiar routine. It remains meaningful communication even before fluent speech.
Why is pointing a useful behavior to describe precisely?~It may request an object or share attention~It always means the same intention~It is purely a grasp reflex~It cannot involve another person's attention~Pointing can serve different functions. Observing whether the child seeks an object or shares interest helps interpret the exchange.
What distinguishes imitation from spontaneous initiation?~Imitation follows a model; initiation begins the exchange~Imitation always involves speech~Initiation requires adult grammar~Both terms mean following written instructions~A child copying a sound and a child beginning an exchange demonstrate different interaction conditions. Both can contribute to learning.
# 5
A toddler says 'juice' to request a drink. What makes it meaningful communication?~The word is used intentionally in a relevant situation~It contains two syllables~It is pronounced without any error~It appears in a printed book~Meaningful use depends on intention and context. A first word need not have adult-perfect pronunciation to communicate successfully.
Which expression most clearly combines two meanings?~More water~Water repeated during unrelated babbling~A single unchanging hum~A startled cry~More water combines a request for additional quantity with the object requested. Early combinations extend beyond naming one thing alone.
What does following a one-step instruction mainly demonstrate?~Understanding a simple directed message~Perfect speech clarity~Independent reading comprehension~Adult-level auditory memory~The task shows comprehension within its context. Gestures, familiarity and attention can also influence whether the instruction is followed.
Why are two related instructions more demanding than one?~The child must retain and sequence additional information~They require no comprehension~They test only voice production~They remove every memory demand~Remembering more than one action increases processing and memory requirements. Familiarity and how the instruction is presented also matter.
Which example is a question conveyed partly through intonation?~A rising 'More?' while offering an empty cup~A silent reflexive hand grasp~A random toe movement~A written full stop~Pitch pattern can help convey a question before complex grammar is available. The interaction and gesture help clarify the intended request.
A child labels a cow as 'moo'. How should this be examined?~Consider its consistent meaningful use in the child's system~Reject it solely because it is not the adult noun~Treat it as proof of a language disorder~Assume it is only a hearing test response~Early forms can be meaningful despite differing from adult vocabulary. Consistency, intention and development over time guide interpretation.
Why is a single vocabulary count insufficient for a bilingual toddler's full profile?~Knowledge and use may be distributed across languages~Only one language can contain meaningful words~Bilingualism eliminates the need for comprehension assessment~Every language has identical naming opportunities~Assessment should consider the child's languages and exposure. One count in only one setting can miss relevant knowledge and communication.
What change is illustrated when a toddler uses words to ask for help instead of only pulling an adult?~An expansion of expressive strategies~A loss of all nonverbal communication~Completion of every language skill~An exclusively auditory reflex~The child has added a linguistic way to communicate a need. Gestures may still remain useful alongside words.
# 6
What does increasing use of pronouns show most directly?~Developing grammatical and referential language~The onset of permanent dentition~A change in body proportions~A complete measure of hearing sensitivity~Pronouns help refer to people and things within interaction. Their use develops alongside other grammatical and conversational abilities.
Which utterance most clearly marks past time?~We played yesterday~We play now~Can we play?~Please play here~Played and yesterday locate the event in the past. Learning tense contributes to talking about events beyond the immediate moment.
Four utterances contain 2, 3, 3 and 4 words. What is their mean length in words?~3 words~2 words~4 words~12 words~Add the words: 2 + 3 + 3 + 4 = 12. Divide by four utterances to obtain a mean of three words; this is not the child's age.
Why must an MLU report specify words or morphemes?~The counting unit changes the calculation~The units are always identical~MLU measures only loudness~Morphemes are units of body length~An inflected word can contain more than one morpheme. Word-based and morpheme-based averages therefore need clearly stated units and methods.
What is intelligibility?~How understandable speech is to a listener~The child's total receptive vocabulary~The number of written pages read~The strength of the jaw alone~Intelligibility concerns listener understanding of spoken output. Familiarity, context, language and the speech sample can influence judgments.
Why may a parent understand a child more easily than an unfamiliar listener?~The parent knows the child's patterns and context~The child must be using two unrelated languages~Familiarity proves a hearing disorder~Unfamiliar listeners cannot assess speech at all~Familiar listeners can use routines and known speech patterns. This is why the identity of the listener matters when reporting intelligibility.
Which task assesses narrative organization more directly than isolated vocabulary?~Retelling an event in a coherent sequence~Naming one familiar object~Reacting to a loud bell~Repeating one vowel~A narrative links events and relationships into a message. It requires organization beyond knowing individual words.
A preschooler takes an idiom literally. What is most appropriate?~Explain the intended meaning using a suitable example~Infer an intellectual diagnosis from one response~Assume literal meanings are never useful~Stop all storytelling~Figurative language adds demands beyond literal vocabulary. A clear example can make the intended meaning accessible without overinterpreting one response.
# 7
What is a synonym?~A word with a similar meaning~A word with an opposite meaning~A sound without any meaning~A sentence with no verb~Synonyms have similar meanings, such as big and large in many contexts. The exact fit still depends on how the word is used.
What is an antonym?~A word with an opposite meaning~A word with the same spelling only~A repeated syllable~A familiar caregiver's name~Antonyms contrast meanings, such as hot and cold. The lecture includes these relationships in later language refinement.
Why can a child manage everyday conversation yet struggle with a textbook explanation?~Academic tasks place different language demands~Conversation proves mastery of every genre~Textbook learning requires no language~The child must lack all comprehension~Academic language may use unfamiliar vocabulary, dense sentences and explanation structures. Conversational competence does not guarantee ease with every school task.
Which sentence shows a causal link in a narrative?~The match stopped because it rained~The match was outside~The players wore blue~There was a ball~Because links an event with its reason. Coherent narratives increasingly explain connections rather than only list observations.
What makes a story cohesive for a listener?~Clear links and references between its parts~The greatest possible number of unrelated words~Constantly changing names without explanation~Only perfect pronunciation of one sound~Cohesion helps listeners follow who or what is being discussed and how events connect. Pronouns and connecting words can contribute when used clearly.
How can oral-language difficulty affect literacy?~It can create challenges with meaning, narratives and written expression~It guarantees every reading outcome~It affects only handwriting pressure~It makes instruction irrelevant~Language supports understanding and organizing written messages. Literacy outcomes also depend on instruction, experience and other relevant abilities.
What distinguishes decoding print from understanding a text?~Reading word forms is not the same as grasping the message~Both are always identical in every reader~Comprehension concerns only pencil movement~Decoding proves full understanding of every sentence~A learner may read words aloud without fully understanding the passage. Meaning requires vocabulary, language processing and relevant knowledge as well.
Why does later understanding of idioms require more than word-by-word interpretation?~The phrase can carry a conventional figurative meaning~Every idiom is a literal physical instruction~Idioms have no communicative function~Only speech loudness changes the meaning~An expression such as break the ice usually refers to easing social tension. Understanding its intended sense goes beyond interpreting each word literally.
# 8
Which adult response is an expansion of 'dog run'?~Yes, the dog is running~No, never speak until it is perfect~Say the alphabet instead~Ignore the message entirely~An expansion preserves the child's meaning while offering a richer form. It models language without turning every attempt into a correction test.
What is a benefit of shared picture-book reading?~It creates meaningful opportunities for talk about shared material~It guarantees every child reads independently at once~It replaces all real-life conversation~It is useful only when the child stays silent~Pictures provide a shared focus for naming, commenting and storytelling. The quality of interaction matters alongside exposure to print.
Why should an adult allow time after asking a question?~The child needs space to process and respond~Silence always proves lack of understanding~Faster adult talk guarantees better learning~The adult should answer every question immediately~A pause gives the child a turn. Constantly filling the gap can prevent the learner from showing what they can communicate.
Which routine offers a natural chance for language learning?~Talking together while preparing a familiar snack~Keeping every daily task silent~Playing unrelated speech continuously in the background~Testing isolated words without meaningful interaction only~Daily routines connect words with actions and needs. The adult can name, respond and expand around an experience both participants understand.
How can language help emotional regulation?~It provides ways to describe feelings and plan actions~It eliminates every feeling of frustration~It replaces the need for supportive adults immediately~It makes all physical cues irrelevant~Words can help children express distress or remind themselves of steps. Support and practice are still needed as regulation develops.
What is private speech?~Speech directed toward oneself to guide activity~Speech that exists only in written examinations~A parent's confidential medical history~A synonym for complete silence~A child may say first this, then that while working. Such self-directed language can help organize attention and behavior.
Why is varied, responsive input more useful than counting adult words alone?~Meaning, engagement and opportunities to respond matter~Only the loudest words are learned~A larger word count guarantees understanding~Children learn nothing from familiar routines~Amount is only one feature of experience. Interaction that connects to a child's focus provides opportunities to interpret and use language.
What should a caregiver do when concerned about communication progress?~Discuss the concern and seek appropriate assessment~Wait indefinitely because variation exists~Diagnose from one vocabulary number~Assume all difficulties are deliberate~Variation does not mean concerns should be ignored. Assessment can examine hearing, comprehension, expression and the child's language context.
A child hears instructions but struggles to remember, explain and write them. What is the best next focus?~Separate auditory access, language and task-memory demands~Judge only the child's pronunciation~Use height as the main explanatory measure~Assume hearing a sound proves every processing skill~Detection, comprehension, memory and expression are different demands. Understanding the task helps identify where support or further assessment is needed.
