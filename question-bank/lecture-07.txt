# 1
Which process belongs most directly to cognition?~Reasoning about how two events are connected~Increasing in body length~Erupting a permanent tooth~Developing a secondary sexual characteristic~Cognition includes acquiring and using knowledge. Reasoning, remembering and interpreting information are examples of mental processes.
Why is cognitive learning described as cumulative?~New understanding can build on earlier experience~Each experience erases previous knowledge~Only chronological age contributes~Learning begins anew every morning~Earlier learning can provide concepts and strategies for new tasks. Knowing how one container opens may help a child explore another.
A child forgets the third step of an instruction while carrying out the first two. Which demand is especially relevant?~Working-memory load~Tooth mineralization~Pubic-hair staging~The rooting response~The task requires holding information while acting on it. Reducing the number of simultaneous steps may make the instruction more manageable.
Which approach reduces unnecessary working-memory demands?~Present manageable steps and relevant visual cues~Give all instructions rapidly without pause~Add unrelated information before each step~Require the child to remember an ever-changing rule~Support can reduce how much information must be held at once. A simple checklist may free attention for performing the task.
A learner remembers many cricket positions but fewer unfamiliar science terms. What may explain this?~Domain-specific expertise~Equal expertise in every subject~A permanent inability to learn science~No effect of prior knowledge~Familiar knowledge helps organize and retrieve relevant information. Strength in one domain does not imply unlimited memory in all domains.
Why should task performance be distinguished from underlying competence?~Instructions, attention and response demands can affect demonstration~A failed task always proves absent ability~Every task measures one isolated process~Context cannot alter performance~A child may understand something yet struggle to show it under a complex task condition. Interpretation should consider what the task requires.
Which observation suggests a strategy rather than rote repetition?~Grouping related items to remember them~Repeating an unfamiliar list without organization~Ignoring the task goal~Guessing without examining the materials~Grouping gives information a useful structure. Strategy use can change performance even when the number of items remains the same.
Why is one cognitive task an incomplete developmental profile?~Different tasks draw on different skills and knowledge~Every task measures all mental processes~The easiest task alone is sufficient~Cognition is unrelated to language and movement~Reasoning tasks vary in language, memory and motor demands. Several observations provide a more complete picture of strengths and needs.
# 2
Which Piagetian stage emphasizes learning through sensory and motor activity?~Sensorimotor~Preoperational~Concrete operational~Formal operational~The sensorimotor stage describes early learning through action and perception. The stage framework is a theoretical account, not an individual diagnostic timetable.
Which Piagetian stage is associated with symbolic thought and prominent egocentrism?~Preoperational~Sensorimotor~Concrete operational~Formal operational~The preoperational account emphasizes symbols and language alongside limits in certain forms of logical reasoning and perspective-taking.
Which stage is associated with logical operations on concrete information?~Concrete operational~Sensorimotor~Preoperational~Formal operational only~Concrete operations involve reasoning about tangible or familiar relationships. Conservation and reversibility are key examples in the classical account.
Which stage is associated with systematic abstract reasoning?~Formal operational~Sensorimotor~Preoperational~Attachment-in-the-making~Formal operations concern reasoning with possibilities and abstract relations. Use of such reasoning still varies by task, knowledge and context.
What does reversibility mean in Piagetian reasoning?~Mentally undoing an action or transformation~Forgetting an action immediately~Repeating a sound automatically~Changing body direction while walking~Reversibility allows a child to think back through a transformation. Water can be imagined being poured back into the original container.
What is centration?~Focusing on one salient feature while overlooking others~Considering every dimension systematically~A perfect balance between all perspectives~Remembering a complete sequence without effort~In a conservation task a child may focus on the height of a glass and overlook its width. This is the classic idea of centration.
In the course's classical stage table, approximately which period is assigned to preoperational thought?~About 2–7 years~Birth to about 2 years~About 7–11 years~Only late adulthood~The table assigns approximately 2–7 years to preoperational thought. This is a theoretical age band; individual task performance should not be judged from age alone.
In the course's classical table, approximately which period is assigned to concrete operations?~About 7–11 years~Only the newborn month~About 2–4 months~Only adulthood after 40~The table uses approximately 7–11 years. These broad bands organize the theory and do not mean every concrete skill appears on one birthday.
# 3
Which sensory modality concerns smell?~Olfactory~Auditory~Visual~Tactile~Olfactory information comes through smell. The lecture includes several sensory routes through which infants encounter and learn about their surroundings.
Which sensory modality concerns touch?~Tactile~Olfactory~Auditory~Visual~Tactile experience concerns touch. Early cognition is supported by multiple senses rather than waiting for spoken explanations to become possible.
An infant responds less after seeing the same display repeatedly. Which learning pattern is relevant?~Habituation~Conservation~Formal reasoning~Animism~Habituation is reduced responding with repetition. Careful studies distinguish it from fatigue or other explanations using an appropriate design.
After repeated exposure, a new display renews an infant's attention. What may this indicate?~Detection of a change or novelty~Adult-like understanding of all objects~A fixed level of general intelligence~The impossibility of prior learning~Renewed attention can be evidence that the new display is discriminated. The conclusion should match the study rather than imply complete conceptual understanding.
Why do infants repeat actions that create an interesting sensation?~Repetition offers experience with action and outcome~The action necessarily has adult intentions~Repetition always shows a disorder~No learning can occur without language~Repeated actions let infants explore predictable effects. Moving a hand and watching it can supply both motor and sensory experience.
A baby watches the hands and touches the cheeks repeatedly. Which process is illustrated?~Exploration of the body and its sensations~Mastery of conservation~A mature theory of social rules~Abstract algebraic reasoning~The infant is learning through bodily activity and perception. Early cognitive exploration includes the child's own movements and sensations.
Why can an increasingly alert infant become distracted during feeding?~More surrounding stimuli may attract attention~Distraction proves no recognition of the caregiver~All feeding must therefore stop permanently~Only teeth determine visual interest~As awareness of surroundings grows, other events may attract attention. Interpret the behavior in the full feeding and developmental context.
What is the limitation of equating longer looking with greater understanding in every study?~Looking can have several explanations depending on the task~Looking time has no research use~Every infant understands every display equally~A longer look directly gives a vocabulary score~Looking measures attention or response patterns. A study must justify what those patterns imply rather than treating them as a complete readout of thought.
# 4
Which situation most clearly requires object permanence?~Searching for a toy hidden under a cloth~Only touching a toy that remains visible~Hearing an unexplained tone~Copying a visible hand movement~The toy is no longer visible but is treated as still existing. Searching can provide evidence, although it also draws on memory and action.
What does occlusion mean in an object-perception task?~An object becoming hidden behind a surface~An object growing physically larger~A sound becoming more intense~A caregiver changing a rule~Occlusion concerns visibility being blocked. Researchers use such events to study expectations about objects that continue behind a barrier.
Why might a reaching task underestimate an infant's object knowledge?~It adds motor planning and memory demands~Reaching requires no action~Infants never learn about hidden objects~A reaching failure always proves no expectation~The infant must show knowledge through a particular response. A demanding motor response can obscure what another task might reveal.
What makes peek-a-boo relevant to the lecture's discussion?~It plays with disappearance and reappearance in interaction~It directly measures skeletal age~It requires formal hypothetical reasoning~It proves all infants have identical memory~The game links hiding and return with a social partner. Enjoyment does not by itself establish a precise research measure of object knowledge.
An infant bangs, drops and passes an object between hands. What is being explored?~Properties and effects of acting on objects~Only the object's adult name~Only social approval from peers~A moral rule about ownership~These actions provide information about sound, movement, texture and handling. Exploration can link perception, movement and emerging expectations.
Why should mouthing be interpreted with both development and safety in mind?~It can be exploration but unsafe objects create risks~It always proves an emotional disorder~All objects are suitable for mouthing~It should be used as the only cognitive test~Mouthing can provide sensory experience. Adults still need safe materials and appropriate supervision; developmental meaning does not remove hazards.
A child searches where a toy was previously hidden rather than its new location. What task demand may matter?~Updating a remembered location and inhibiting an earlier response~Only recognizing the toy's colour~Only knowing the word toy~Only the strength of a newborn grasp~Search performance can reflect memory and response-control demands. It should not be interpreted as a single pure measure without considering the task.
Which conclusion best handles different object-permanence findings across methods?~Methods may reveal different aspects and demands~Every method must produce identical ages~Only one task can contain any evidence~Different findings prove infants never learn~Searching and looking-time methods require different responses. Comparing their demands helps interpret apparently different developmental findings.

Why does increased mobility broaden cognitive opportunities?~It lets the child explore more objects and relationships~It eliminates the need for attention~It proves every problem can now be solved~It replaces language learning~Movement changes what the child can encounter and act upon. New opportunities can contribute to learning about spaces, objects and people.

What shows functional use of an object?~Using a toy brush to brush a doll's hair~Only dropping the brush repeatedly~Only naming its colour~Only watching someone carry it~Functional use reflects knowledge of what an object is used for. A toddler sorts red objects together despite different shapes. Which feature guides the sorting?~Colour~Shape~Weight necessarily~Object name necessarily~The grouping uses one shared property. Sorting tasks help observe which feature the child attends to under a particular instruction.

What extra demand is involved?~Changing the basis of classification~Only holding an object still~Maintaining one unchanging response~Recognizing the sound of a bell~Switching rules requires attending to a new property and moving away from the previous rule. A toddler uses a stick to bring a distant toy closer. What does understanding a part–whole relationship involve?~Recognizing how components belong to a larger object~Assuming every small item is unrelated~Counting only without identifying any object~Reacting to a sound's loudness~A wheel is part of a toy vehicle. Why should puzzle success be interpreted with the puzzle itself in mind?~Piece design, familiarity and assistance change difficulty~Every five-piece puzzle has identical demands~Piece count alone measures intelligence perfectly~Prior experience cannot affect performance~Puzzles differ in visual cues, fit and complexity. What is the classic three-mountains task used to explore?~Visual perspective-taking~Infant feeding coordination~Adult tooth development~Pubertal growth velocity~The task asks about a view different from the child's own. Its language and response demands also matter when interpreting performance.
Why can role play support learning?~It lets children practise meanings and social roles~It requires no coordination with others~It proves fantasy and reality are identical~It replaces every form of instruction~Pretend scenarios offer opportunities for language, negotiation and exploring roles. What distinguishes an extended pretend scenario from a single imitation?~It organizes several related actions or roles~It contains no symbols~It must be copied exactly from an adult~It is possible only after adulthood~A single pretend sip can develop into a tea-party sequence with roles. Why is play a potentially useful outlet for working through experiences?~It permits expression and alternative responses within a scenario~Every play event has one hidden diagnostic meaning~Play eliminates the need for adult support~Imagination proves a child cannot understand reality~Play can let children express and revisit experiences. Which observation would best show cooperative pretend play?~Children negotiate roles and sustain a shared scenario~Children silently use separate toys without interaction~One child watches a fixed picture alone~An infant responds to palm stimulation~A shared pretend scenario requires coordinating meanings with others.
