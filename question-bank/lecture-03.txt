# 1
Why does reaching for a cup require both gross and fine motor control?~Posture stabilizes the body while the hand acts~Fine motor control replaces trunk stability~Reaching involves only finger muscles~Gross and fine systems cannot cooperate~Stable posture supports accurate hand use. The task illustrates why movement domains should not be studied as completely independent systems.
Which change reflects differentiation of movement?~A broad arm movement becomes a controlled finger action~Every movement becomes less specific~A purposeful reach returns permanently to random motion~All body parts always move together~Differentiation means movement becomes more specific and controlled. Selecting an object with fingers differs from a generalized whole-arm response.
What is the most defensible use of a motor sequence?~A framework for observing patterns and progression~A guarantee of one exact timetable~A substitute for observing movement quality~A diagnosis from one missing item~Sequences help organize observation. The child's rate, opportunities, quality of movement and overall development must also be considered.
Which example best illustrates voluntary motor control?~Intentionally letting go of a block into a container~A grasp automatically elicited by palm stimulation~A reflexive trunk curve during examination~An involuntary startle pattern~Voluntary control involves goal-directed action. Releasing a block where intended differs from an automatically elicited response.
In the lecture's approximate sequence, copying a circle is associated with which age?~About 3 years~About 6 months~About 12 months~About 8 years~The slides place circle copying around age three, before square and triangle copying. This is a course teaching anchor, not a diagnostic deadline for an individual child.
Which description belongs to gross motor control?~Maintaining balance while changing body position~Using fingertips to turn one page~Rotating a small lid between fingers~Making a precise mark on paper~Gross motor control includes balance, posture and large movements. Fine tasks involve more precise hand and finger manipulation.
Why is safe practice important after biological readiness emerges?~Skills become more coordinated through use~Readiness guarantees expert performance immediately~Practice determines every developmental outcome~Only inherited traits affect coordination~Maturation enables new actions, and opportunities help refine them. A new walker still practises balance, stopping and turning.
In the lecture's approximate sequence, copying a square is associated with which age?~About 4 years~About 9 months~About 18 months~About 7 years~The slides place square copying around age four, following circle copying and preceding triangle copying. Which posture is described by the term prone?~Lying on the abdomen~Lying on the back~Sitting upright~Standing with support~Prone means on the abdomen, while supine means on the back. These positions provide different views of postural control.
Which posture is described by the term supine?~Lying on the back~Lying on the abdomen~Kneeling upright~Sitting without support~Supine is a back-lying position. Knowing position terms helps students interpret the lecture's descriptions of infant movement.
What does head lag describe during a trained assessment?~The head trailing behind the trunk during a position change~The head remaining aligned with the trunk~The ability to turn toward a voice~The use of both hands together~Head lag concerns relative head and trunk control. Interpretation is age- and context-dependent, and assessment should be performed safely by a trained person.
Which broad progression is described in the lecture's prone observations?~Forearm support toward support through more extended arms~Independent walking before any head control~Precision finger grasp before all arm movement~Permanent loss of trunk control with age~The lecture traces increasing antigravity control. Support through forearms can progress toward support through the hands as strength and coordination develop.
How does supported sitting differ from independent sitting?~External assistance still helps maintain the position~Supported sitting requires no trunk control~Independent sitting always includes walking~The two terms describe the same assistance level~The amount of support matters when recording a skill. Brief sitting while held is different from maintaining balance without that assistance.
An infant twists while sitting to reach a toy. What added ability is being observed?~Dynamic control within the sitting position~Only passive support from an adult~A feeding reflex alone~Completion of all gross motor development~Maintaining sitting during reaching requires more than remaining still. Dynamic balance helps the child act within a posture.
Which task is associated with about 5–6 months in the lecture's gross-motor sequence?~Sitting with support and increasing hand support in prone~Skipping on alternating feet~Independent alternating stair descent~Pedalling a tricycle~The slides describe increasing prone support and supported sitting in this broad period. Support level matters, and the age label is an approximate course anchor.
Which observation is associated with about 8–9 months in the lecture's sequence?~Sitting without support and pivoting~Skipping fluently~Independent hopping over obstacles~Drawing a square~The lecture places unsupported sitting and pivoting in this approximate period. This does not mean waiting until a listed month if a family has concerns about progress.
# 3
Which new demand becomes important after a child first walks independently?~Controlling speed and direction~Learning the rooting response~Maintaining an obligatory grasp reflex~Avoiding every change of surface~Walking skill continues to develop beyond first steps. Stopping, turning and adapting to surroundings require further balance and practice.
A toddler uses both feet on each stair before moving to the next. What is this pattern?~A step-to pattern~An alternating-foot pattern~Skipping~Cruising~In a step-to pattern both feet reach one step. Alternating feet places one foot on each successive step and demands different coordination.
What detail is essential when comparing two children's stair skills?~Whether they use a rail or adult support~Only the staircase colour~Only which child is heavier~Whether they name the steps aloud~Support substantially changes stair demands. A child using a rail and one stepping unaided should not be treated as performing identical tasks.
Which task places a particularly clear demand on single-leg balance?~Hopping~Sitting with back support~Rolling from back to side~Holding a cup with two hands~Hopping involves taking off and landing on one leg. It therefore differs from a two-foot jump and requires single-leg balance.
How does a jump differ from a hop in basic movement terminology?~A hop uses one foot; a basic two-foot jump uses both~A hop always means sliding on the floor~A jump is exclusively a hand movement~Both terms necessarily mean alternating stair steps~The support and landing pattern distinguishes the tasks. Clear terminology helps interpret gross motor descriptions accurately.
Why does pedalling a tricycle require more than leg strength?~It also needs coordinated action and steering~It depends only on tooth eruption~It requires no balance or attention~It proves all later sport skills are mature~Pedalling combines coordination, steering and attention with force production. Complex movement cannot be reduced to one muscle property.
Which movement appears around two years in the lecture's approximate sequence?~Running and kicking a ball~Alternating-foot skipping~A fluent single-leg hop~Independent tricycle steering in traffic~The slides associate running and kicking with roughly two years. Observe control, opportunity and safety rather than treating the label as a stand-alone diagnostic cutoff.
How should the lecture's ages for skipping or stair alternation be used?~As approximate teaching anchors with contextual observation~As universal pass–fail birthdays~As exact intelligence cutoffs~As rules that override every clinical concern~Complex skills vary with maturation and practice. The age labels help organize learning but cannot alone establish a diagnosis.
# 4
Which foundational hand skill is most involved in placing a block deliberately on a table?~Controlled release~Rooting~Moro response~Head lag~Controlled release means letting go intentionally at a chosen point. It supports placing, building and many self-care tasks.
What does wrist rotation help a child do?~Adjust the hand's orientation during manipulation~Replace all finger movement~Determine bone age directly~Maintain only a newborn flexed posture~Wrist rotation helps orient an object or tool. Turning a handle, for example, involves directing the hand as well as grasping.
Why are reaching and grasping distinct?~Reaching brings the hand toward an object; grasping secures it~Reaching always follows release~Grasping means looking without touching~Both refer only to passive reflexes~An effective reach positions the hand, while a grasp holds the object. A child may show different levels of control in these related actions.
What is bilateral coordination?~Using both hands together in related roles~Using either hand with no relation to the task~Moving only one finger repeatedly~Avoiding use of the nonpreferred hand~Many tasks require cooperation between hands. One hand may hold a container steady while the other opens it.
A child can reach accurately but cannot adjust the grasp to a handle. What needs closer observation?~How the hand shapes and secures the object~Only lower-limb endurance~Only understanding of colour names~The assumption that reaching proves all hand control~Successful reaching does not establish every manipulation skill. Grasp selection and adjustment add demands once the hand reaches the object.
Why is vision relevant to fine motor activity?~It can guide the hand toward and within the task~It is the only influence on hand control~It makes posture unnecessary~It guarantees precise manipulation in every child~Visual information helps locate and monitor objects. Fine motor performance also draws on posture, sensation, experience and coordination.
Which task best demonstrates stabilizing and manipulating roles?~Holding paper while cutting with the other hand~Kicking a ball forward~Running in a straight line~Lying in a flexed posture~One hand stabilizes material while the other operates a tool. This division of roles is important in many practical hand activities.
Why should practice objects be chosen carefully for infants?~Small detachable items may be choking hazards~Every household item is equally safe~The smallest item always teaches best~Motor learning requires unrestricted access to sharp tools~Developmental practice must suit both the skill and safety needs. Safe size, material and supervision matter more than copying a demonstration with unsuitable objects.

What does hand regard mean in early development?~Looking at one's own hands~Measuring finger length~Using a mature pencil grasp~Naming each finger~Hand regard involves visual attention to the hands. It is an early observation linking emerging visual and motor experience.
An infant moves a toy from one hand to the other. Which ability is demonstrated?~Object transfer~Mature handwriting~Alternating stair climbing~A plantar response~Transfer involves coordinating the hands around the same object. It contributes to exploration and later manipulation.
How does a precision grasp differ from a whole-hand grasp?~It uses more differentiated finger control~It always requires both feet~It is purely a visual response~It prevents deliberate release~Precision requires finer control of the digits. Why is finding a hidden object not purely a fine motor test?~It also involves memory, attention and object knowledge~It measures only finger strength~It excludes visual processing~It is unrelated to task understanding~Searching requires knowing or remembering where to act as well as reaching. A task can combine cognitive and motor demands.
Which development is illustrated by turning one page rather than several at once?~More differentiated hand control~A return to reflexive grasping~Loss of object permanence~A new gross motor posture~Selecting a single page involves controlling contact and force. It illustrates refinement rather than a completely separate body system.
How does spontaneous scribbling differ from scribbling only after demonstration?~The child initiates it without immediately copying a model~It necessarily uses smaller muscles~It always produces recognizable letters~It proves age-appropriate reading~Initiation and imitation are different task conditions. In the lecture's teaching sequence, a tower of roughly six to seven blocks is placed near which age?~About 2 years~About 3 months~About 6 months~About 5 years~The course places this block-building task around two years. Which manual task does the lecture place near two years?~Turning a doorknob or unscrewing a lid~Only visually regarding the hands~Only holding a rattle placed in the hand~Mastering every shoelace sequence~The course associates turning handles and lids with this approximate period. What does developmental deviancy refer to in the lecture's framework?~An atypical pattern or quality of development~Any cultural difference in clothing~The number of skills recorded~Every instance of ordinary frustration~Deviancy here is a technical description of an unusual developmental pattern.
