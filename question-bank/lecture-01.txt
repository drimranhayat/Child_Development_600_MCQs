# 1
A chart records a child's weight rising from 12 kg to 14 kg. What kind of evidence is this?~Quantitative growth~Qualitative social change~Moral reasoning~Emotional regulation~Weight is a numerical body measurement. The change describes growth; it does not by itself tell us how the child communicates or relates to others.
Which change best demonstrates increased functional capacity?~Learning to fasten a coat~An increase in foot length~A rise in body weight~An increase in head circumference~Development includes what a person can do. Fastening a coat shows a new functional skill, whereas the other options describe body measurements.
How are growth and development best assessed together?~Measure size and observe abilities over time~Use weight as the only indicator~Observe abilities but disregard health~Compare one measurement with a sibling~Physical measurements and functional observations answer different questions. Together they show how a child is growing and managing everyday tasks.
Why does development include both qualitative and quantitative change?~Abilities can change in kind and amount~Every change can be reduced to height~Only unmeasurable changes count~Development excludes numerical information~A child may use more words and also begin organizing them into sentences. There is a change in quantity and in the structure of communication.
Two children have the same height but different language abilities. What follows?~Equal size does not imply equal skills~Their development must be identical~Height invalidates language observation~The shorter vocabulary proves poor nutrition~Body size and language are related to different aspects of development. Similar growth measurements do not establish identical functional profiles.
A study records both stable temperament and newly acquired skills. Why is it developmental?~The field examines stability as well as change~Only changing traits belong in the field~Stable traits cannot be observed scientifically~A stable trait rules out later development~Human development investigates continuities and changes. A consistent cautious approach can coexist with gains in language and independence.
Which observation would best complement a child's height record?~How the child manages age-relevant tasks~The height of a classroom door~The manufacturer's scale label~One adult's unsupported prediction~Functional observation adds information beyond size. Watching communication, movement and self-care helps describe development more fully.
Which statement about cell number and cell size is sound?~Both can contribute to physical growth~Only cell size can change body size~Cell division is a social process~Neither relates to physical growth~Growth may involve more cells, larger cells, or both. These mechanisms concern physical change rather than the entire developmental profile.

What does developmental modifiability imply for teaching?~Support and experience can change performance~Abilities are completely fixed at birth~Any difficulty disappears without support~Teaching produces identical outcomes in everyone~Modifiability means change remains possible. Which example best shows historical context affecting development?~A school closure changing learning opportunities~A child growing one centimetre~A grasp response during examination~A single inherited blood group~Historical events alter the conditions in which children learn. A child devotes more effort to learning new skills; an older adult focuses on maintaining independence. Which idea fits?~Resource allocation changes across life~Resources are allocated identically at every age~Maintenance is outside development~Only children have developmental goals~People allocate time and effort differently across life. Development includes acquiring, maintaining and adapting abilities.
Why does culture belong in a lifespan account?~It shapes expectations and learning opportunities~It determines every biological event~It removes individual variation~It affects adults but never children~Culture influences valued skills, routines and opportunities. For example, expectations about children's household responsibilities differ across communities.
Which conclusion follows from lifelong development?~Adults can continue adapting to new roles~Adulthood guarantees psychological completion~Childhood experience becomes irrelevant~All adult change is physical decline~Roles and demands continue changing in adulthood. Becoming a parent or learning a profession involves psychological adjustment and learning.
A learner improves after task instructions are adapted. What does this most directly challenge?~The assumption that observed performance is unchangeable~The importance of observing the task~The possibility of individual differences~The influence of teaching methods~Improvement after support shows why one performance should not be treated as a permanent limit. Instruction and context can reveal additional capacity.
In the lecture's lifespan overview, which band is labelled young adulthood?~About 20–40 years~Birth to 2 years~About 6–12 years~About 65 years onward~The course overview labels roughly 20–40 years as young adulthood. These broad social-developmental periods organize the syllabus rather than define identical life events for everyone.

In the lecture's period scheme, conception to birth is called what?~The prenatal period~Toddlerhood~Early childhood~Middle childhood~Prenatal means before birth. It includes major physical and neurological changes and the prenatal context of later development.
Which period follows infancy and toddlerhood in the course overview?~Early childhood~Middle adulthood~Late adulthood~The prenatal period~The overview moves from prenatal life to infancy and toddlerhood, then early childhood. These categories organize study rather than impose identical birthdays for every skill.
Why can a child's developmental profile be more useful than one overall label?~It shows strengths and needs across domains~It assumes every domain is identical~It replaces all contextual information~It ranks the child's moral worth~A profile can show strong movement alongside language support needs. This helps select relevant opportunities and further assessment.
Which task most directly demonstrates gross motor participation?~Climbing onto a low step~Threading a large bead~Describing a picture~Naming an emotion~Gross motor activities use larger movements and posture. Climbing requires balance and coordinated movement of the trunk and limbs.
Which task most directly demonstrates adaptive independence?~Managing a familiar handwashing routine~Increasing in body length~Showing a newborn reflex~Developing a permanent molar~Adaptive skills concern daily demands in context. Handwashing combines learned steps with practical self-care.
A student assumes every 12-year-old enters the same psychological stage on their birthday. What is the error?~Treating broad periods as fixed individual boundaries~Recognizing that age is relevant~Considering physical and social change~Distinguishing childhood from adulthood~Developmental periods are broad teaching categories. In the lecture's lifespan overview, which band is labelled middle adulthood?~About 40–65 years~Birth to 2 years~About 2–6 years~About 12–19 years~The supplied overview uses roughly 40–65 years for middle adulthood. # 4
What happens during synaptic pruning?~Some connections are selectively reduced~All learning-related connections vanish~Neuron numbers necessarily double each day~The brain becomes independent of experience~Pruning is selective reorganization. Connections that are used and those that are less used can follow different developmental paths.
Why is repeated, responsive play relevant to brain development?~It provides patterned opportunities to learn~It guarantees a fixed intelligence score~It replaces nutrition and sleep~It finishes all brain maturation immediately~Repeated exchanges give the child practice in attention, prediction and interaction. They support learning alongside health, nutrition and maturation.
Which combination best reflects the lecture's multiple-input view?~Nutrition, health, responsive care and safety~Nutrition without interaction~Stimulation without protection~Academic drilling without basic care~The lecture emphasizes coordinated support. A child needs opportunities to learn together with adequate care, health and a safe environment.
A well-fed child rarely receives responsive interaction. What is missing from the support plan?~Opportunities for reciprocal learning~All biological maturation~Every capacity for future improvement~A guarantee of advanced academic ability~Nutrition supports the developing body, but interaction creates additional learning opportunities. Adequate feeding alone does not provide conversation or shared play.
Why is the statement 'the number of synapses stays constant' misleading?~Connections are formed and reorganized over time~All connections disappear at school entry~Experience cannot affect connections~Pruning affects only body height~Development includes connection formation and selective reorganization. The balance changes across regions and periods rather than remaining universally constant.
What is the most appropriate interpretation of a sensitive period?~Some experiences have especially strong effects at certain times~Learning is impossible outside one short interval~Every child has the same exact timetable~Care after infancy has no value~A sensitive period highlights timing-related opportunity or vulnerability. It does not justify abandoning support later in development.
A child is repeatedly frightened during learning activities. Which lecture principle is most relevant?~Safety and emotional support contribute to learning conditions~Fear guarantees stronger learning~Health and protection are separate from development~Only the number of worksheets matters~Children learn within bodily and emotional conditions. Predictable, supportive interaction can help them attend and participate.
Which statement best integrates nature and nurture?~Experience acts within developing biological systems~Genes work without any environment~Experience replaces all biological processes~Only one influence can matter at a time~Biological readiness and experience interact. For example, a developing communication system benefits from opportunities to hear or see meaningful language.

Why should a biological influence not be treated as destiny?~Outcomes also depend on other influences and support~Biology has no role in development~Every exposure produces identical effects~Support erases all individual differences~A risk factor changes probabilities, not a person's entire future with certainty. How can hormonal influences be understood in the lecture?~They help regulate biological and behavioral changes~They determine every thought directly~They eliminate cultural influences~They affect growth but no other process~Hormones participate in regulation and maturation. Which question best investigates an environmental hazard?~Has the child been exposed to harmful substances?~Which sibling is tallest?~What is the child's favourite story?~Which colour is used for the growth chart?~Exposure history can identify relevant risks. Which observation concerns attachment rather than general sociability?~Seeking a particular caregiver when distressed~Smiling equally at every picture~Enjoying a colourful mobile~Reaching for every nearby object~Attachment concerns a relationship with a particular person.
